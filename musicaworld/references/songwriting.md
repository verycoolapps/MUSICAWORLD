# Songwriting Reference

Load for original songs: structure, lyrics, hooks, vocal writing.

## Song Architecture

Standard pop/rock form — use as vocabulary, not cage:

```
INTRO → VERSE 1 → PRE-CHORUS → CHORUS →
VERSE 2 → PRE-CHORUS → CHORUS →
BRIDGE/BREAKDOWN → FINAL CHORUS (+OUTRO)
```

Variants worth knowing:
- **Through-composed**: no repeated sections (art song, progressive).
- **Strophic**: same melody, new lyrics (folk, ballad).
- **ABABCB**: verse-chorus-verse-chorus-bridge-chorus.
- **Post-chorus**: short lift section after chorus hook.
- **Pre-chorus**: tension ramp — rising sequence, snare build, lyric pivot.

## Hook Engineering

The hook is the song's contract. Requirements:
- 1–2 melodic gestures, rhythmically confident, in singable range.
- Lyric hook: short phrase (≤8 syllables ideally), concrete image or
  emotional thesis, natural stress on strong beats.
- Contrast: hook must differ from verse in register, density, or rhythm —
  if verse and chorus feel same-y, the chorus isn't a chorus.
- Test: strip everything but hook — does it still work a cappella?

## Lyric Craft — Anti-Cliché Protocol

FORBIDDEN:
- Empty motivation ("believe in yourself", "never give up")
- Predictable end-rhymes on every line
- Generic romantic metaphors (fire, stars, oceans of love)
- Random poetic words with no semantic job
- Artificially complex vocabulary
- Chorus repeating one meaningless phrase on loop

REQUIRED:
- Specific imagery: objects, places, actions — not abstractions
- Authentic emotional perspective (who speaks? to whom? why now?)
- Natural language: how people actually talk/sing, with intentional craft
- Syllable control: map stressed syllables to strong beats; flag lines
  where stress fights melody
- Rhyme architecture: mix exact/near/slant/meter-only; place strongest
  rhyme at section ends
- Narrative or emotional arc across sections — verse adds information,
  chorus pays it off

## Multilingual Lyrics

- Default: **Indonesian**. Natural idioms > literal translation.
- English, Japanese, Korean supported; honor each language's prosody:
  - Indonesian: syllable-timed, vowel-rich, natural for melisma.
  - Japanese: mora-timed, pitch accent matters for melody fit.
  - Korean: syllable blocks, tense/lax consonants affect attack feel.
- For mixed-language songs, keep each section single-language unless
  code-switching is the artistic point; flag pronunciation risks.

## Vocal Direction

Specify per section: register, intensity, style (breathy, belted, falsetto,
staccato, legato), doubles/choir treatment, ad-libs placement.
Describe what the voice *does*, not just what it sings.

## Melody Specification Format

When notating hooks/motifs textually:

```
Key: D minor | BPM: 92 | Meter: 4/4
HOOK (bars 9–12):
  bar 9:  A4(8) C5(8) D5(4) — "kau tinggalkan" 
  bar 10: E5(4) D5(4) C5(8) A4(8) — "bayangan di lorong"
  scale degrees: 5 7 1 2 1 7 5 — ascending lift, fall home
```

Scale degrees + rhythm + lyric alignment = reproducible melody spec.

## Song Deliverable Checklist

Title · concept (1–2 sentences) · genre/hybrid · emotional identity ·
BPM/key/meter · full lyrics with section labels · structure map ·
vocal direction per section · instrumentation · arrangement arc ·
production direction · AI-generation prompt · quality score (§gate).
