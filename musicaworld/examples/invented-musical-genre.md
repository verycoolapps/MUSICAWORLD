# Invented Musical Genre — "Hollow Waltz"

> Complete worked specimen: fully specified invented genre per
> `references/genre-innovation.md` 10-point mutation protocol,
> plus one example track sketch.

## 1. Name

**Hollow Waltz** (Indonesian: *Waltz Berongga*)

Etymology: "hollow" = the space inside the music (what's left out),
"waltz" = the 3/4 dance form being hollowed out.

## 2. Philosophy

**"Dance with the absence."** The Hollow Waltz takes the Waltz —
history's most romantic dance form — and removes its melody,
harmony, and pulse, leaving only the *space* where music should be.
The listener completes the music themselves.

## 3. Emotional Identity

**Nostalgic loneliness made elegant.** Not sad — *aching*. The
feeling of dancing alone in a ballroom after everyone has left.
Elegance without companionship. The waltz is remembered, not
experienced.

## 4. Rhythmic Characteristics

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Meter** | 3/4 (waltz form) |
| **Tempo range** | 60–90 BPM (slow to moderate) |
| **Pulse** | Weak — the "ONE" is implied, not struck. No drums. |
| **Groove DNA** | Circular sway, not forward drive. The music rotates, doesn't progress. |
| **Rhythmic device** | Hemiola between 3/4 and 6/8 feel — the waltz and the barcarolle coexist |
| **Signature rhythm** | `1 – – 2 – – 3 – –` with bass note on 1 only, chord tones on 2–3 as echoes |

## 5. Harmonic Characteristics

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Mode** | Dorian (minor with raised 6th — hopeful but haunting) |
| **Scale** | D Dorian, but melody uses only 5 notes: D – E – F – A – C |
| **Chord vocabulary** | i – IV – bVII – bIII (modal, unresolved) |
| **Cadence** | Never resolves. Every phrase ends on the dominant or a suspended chord. |
| **Tension device** | Tritone between melody and bass (D against Ab) — the "hollow" interval |
| **Signature progression** | `Dm – Gm – Bb – F` (i – iv – bVI – bIII, all minor, all unresolved) |

## 6. Instrumentation

**Core palette** (3–4 elements only — sparseness is the point):

| Instrument | Role | Processing |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub bass** | Single sustained note per bar | Sine/triangle, 30–60Hz, felt not heard |
| **Glass harmonica** | Melody — sparse, long tones | Granular shimmer, slow attack |
| **Prepared piano** | Harmony — muted strings, single notes | Felt-muted, no sustain pedal |
| **Breath** | Texture — filtered noise, 0.2Hz LFO | Sounds like someone breathing nearby |

**Forbidden**: drums, strings, brass, vocals, electronic beats, anything with a pulse.

## 7. Production Identity

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Texture** | Airy, spacious, lots of silence between notes |
| **Reverb** | Large hall (4–5s decay), 100ms pre-delay — the ballroom is empty |
| **Loudness** | Very quiet — −20 to −23 LUFS. The music should feel distant. |
| **Stereo** | Wide but sparse — instruments orbit slowly, never center-clustered |
| **Dynamic range** | Extreme — pp to mp only. No loud moments. |
| **Noise floor** | Constant low-level room tone (breath, air) — the space is alive |

## 8. Vocal Characteristics

**Optional — wordless only.** If vocals appear:

- **Style**: breathy, wordless humming or sighing
- **Range**: alto or countertenor (androgynous, distant)
- **Language**: none — phonemes only, like a voice remembering a language
- **Processing**: heavy reverb, pitch drift ±20 cents, sounds like it's coming from another room
- **Rule**: vocals never sing a melody — they *shadow* the glass harmonica a perfect fourth below

## 9. Arrangement Principles

**Typical structure** (3–5 minutes):

```
INTRO (4 bars): sub bass alone, single note, establishes key
A (8 bars): glass harmonica enters, sparse melody
B (8 bars): prepared piano adds harmony, still sparse
A' (8 bars): melody returns, slightly varied, breath texture thickens
C (8 bars): peak — all elements, but still quiet; the "dance"
OUTRO (4 bars): everything drops except sub bass; single note decay
```

**Energy map**: flat — no builds, no drops, no climax. The music
*sways*, it doesn't *climb*. Peak is "more present," not "louder."

## 10. Distinctive Signature

**The missing melody note.** Every phrase ends on a note that *should*
resolve but doesn't — the listener's ear supplies the resolution
mentally, creating a perpetual sense of "almost." This is the genre's
fingerprint: **music that completes itself in the listener's mind."

## Mutation Protocol Trace

| Parent | Element borrowed |
|---|---|
| Waltz | 3/4 meter, circular sway, romantic form |
| Ambient drone | Sustained tones, no pulse, spaciousness |
| Music box | Glass harmonica timbre, music-box register |

**Resolution rule**: Waltz's forward momentum is removed — the music
rotates in place. Ambient's lack of melody is resolved by the glass
harmonica's sparse phrases. Music box's sweetness is darkened by
Dorian mode and sub-bass weight.

## Example Track Sketch — "Salon After Midnight"

### Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Title** | "Salon After Midnight" |
| **Duration** | 3:36 |
| **BPM** | 72 |
| **Key/Mode** | D Dorian |
| **Meter** | 3/4 |
| **Context** | A ballroom after the party — chairs pushed in, candles burned low |

### Melody (glass harmonica)

```
Bar 1: D5 (half note) – F5 (quarter) – E5 (quarter)
Bar 2: D5 (half note) – C5 (quarter) – A4 (quarter)
Bar 3: D5 (dotted half) — rests on the "missing" resolution
Bar 4: F5 (half) – E5 (quarter) – D5 (quarter) — lands, but on dominant

The melody never plays the "1" (D) at phrase end — always stops on
A (dominant) or C (mediant), leaving the resolution to the listener.
```

### Bass (sub)

```
Bar 1: D2 (whole note, 4s decay)
Bar 2: D2 (whole note)
Bar 3: Bb1 (whole note) — bVI color
Bar 4: A1 (whole note) — dominant, unresolved
```

### Harmony (prepared piano)

```
Bar 1: Dm add9 (D-F-A-C-E) — open, airy
Bar 2: Gm add9 (G-Bb-D-F-A) — iv, minor
Bar 3: Bb major (Bb-D-F) — bVI, borrowed
Bar 4: A7sus (A-D-E-G) — dominant suspended, never resolves
```

### Breath texture

```
Constant filtered noise (bandpass 400–1200Hz), amplitude LFO at 0.2Hz
(slow breathing), stereo width ±30% slow pan
```

### Structure

| Section | Bars | Elements | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–4 | sub bass only | 1 |

| B | 13–20 | + prepared piano | 3 |

| C | 29–36 | all elements, still quiet | 4 |

## One-Sentence Test

Describe to a musician: *"A waltz with no drums, no melody resolution,
and only four instruments — sub bass, glass harmonica, muted piano,
and breath — in D Dorian at 72 BPM."* If they can predict the sound:
a lonely ballroom, empty but elegant — the identity is legible.
